## Goal interpretation and trace model

The **11 goals are approved top-level System Requirements with the role “safety goal”**, using the existing [requirement model](../Requirements/REQ-001_Requirements.md#requirement-types-and-architecture-model). That role introduces no fourth requirement type, hardware/software allocation or Unit Requirement. They are maintained here as the canonical concept-phase work product in SG-001-R1.0, outside the separate REQ-001-R1.6 cluster count. This release adopts the goals as an additional controlled baseline; later safety-derived children and further allocation require a controlled requirements revision. Preserve these IDs and link to the canonical goals instead of duplicating their text.

All goals apply within the vehicle scenarios and foreseeable cases identified by HARA-001. `LE-VEH`, its fitted supplied pack and retained mechanical controls own the stated person outcomes. Regeneration remains a vehicle charge-acceptance condition.

**No formal ASIL is assigned** under PD CON-015. Functional goals address the E/E causal routes; broader goals/facets cover mechanical, chemical and nominal-behavior risks under PD §3.3. The event list preserves that distinction. Each goal inherits all cited event situations and their approved qualitative risk judgments, retained assumptions and open evidence gates; combining events does not reduce their required treatment.

“Prevent hazardous…” states the required item-level outcome. It is not an absolute zero-risk demonstration or a command-level implementation. The functional safety concept must derive measurable torque, timing, energy, temperature, retention and visibility criteria. The candidate safe outcomes below describe what the person experiences; none selects a contactor, sensor, watchdog, brake channel, algorithm or power-conversion topology.

**Trace direction:** PD/ID and a hazardous event justify a goal; a later safety requirement derives from that goal; architecture and evidence demonstrate its implementation. Existing requirements listed here **contribute intent or constrain the solution**. They are not retroactively declared fully derived from or sufficient for a safety goal. Source/provenance and derivation remain separate relations. Reverse trace is obtained by searching stable IDs in these two documents and the linked clusters.

## Safe-state interactions and timing

There is **no universal “power off = safe” state**. The concept must reconcile all affected goals and verify the transition as well as the final condition.

| Situation | Required joint reasoning |
|---|---|
| Unintended positive/negative torque | Remove the hazardous wheel effect within its derived time/torque envelope while preserving steering/mechanical braking ([SG-001](#sg-001), [SG-002](#sg-002), [SG-005](#sg-005), [SG-006](#sg-006)). Check the loss-of-drive/deceleration consequence ([SG-003](#sg-003), [SG-004](#sg-004)); a healthy command path is not assumed under output failure. |
| Regen forbidden or battery path interrupted | Protect the pack and generated-energy paths ([SG-008](#sg-008), [SG-010](#sg-010)), prevent unintended drag ([SG-002](#sg-002)), and preserve mechanical stopping control ([SG-004](#sg-004), [SG-006](#sg-006)). Continuing unsafe charge is not an acceptable means of preserving braking. |
| Riding fault, shutdown or exhausted usable energy | Preserve the released inhibition/restart semantics and derive physical energy and visibility behavior. Available auxiliaries do not justify harmful deep discharge, nor does supply removal establish disappearance of residual/wheel-generated energy. |
| Service / battery handling | Establish physical isolation or an explicitly controlled energized task, including wheel generation, before relying on access. A successful diagnostic or completed service task neither grants Ready nor starts a new charging session. |

**Timing derivation:** no FTTI, diagnostic interval or tolerated residual torque/energy is selected here. For each relevant event, derive the earliest hazardous outcome under the declared load/grip/clearance/energy condition; allocate sensing age, communication, diagnosis, decision, command and physical response plus uncertainty within that interval. Verify the total bound and safe-state duration/recovery. Where no dependable time-to-harm or detection is available, derive prevention or physical protection instead of inventing a detection deadline. Existing protocol periods and wire times are constraints, not safety response evidence. This extends the approved [interface timing budget](../Requirements/System_Requirements/Interface_Qualification.md) under HA-OI-002, HA-OI-003, HA-OI-004.

## Released baseline and next work product

Owner review of the analysis and goal statements is complete under HA-OI-006; HA-OI-001 retains factual context confirmation while the conservative analysis assumptions remain effective. Derive the **functional safety concept**: scenario-specific safe states, functional safety requirements, fault response/recovery and timing, responsibilities/interfaces, assumptions about retained/external measures and a validation strategy. Extend the existing requirement derivation graph through a controlled revision; a software child or an E/E-only analysis cannot satisfy the complete physical/broader goal by itself.

The analysis identifies the goals and their source/support/gap trace. HA-OI-002–005 are downstream evidence gates, not missing owner preferences or a claim that physical safety has been demonstrated. Document approval alone cannot close them.
