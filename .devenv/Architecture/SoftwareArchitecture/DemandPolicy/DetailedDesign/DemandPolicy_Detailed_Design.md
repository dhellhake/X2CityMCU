# DD-001 — Software unit design: DemandPolicy

## Caller supplied execution time

Invocation timing follows the canonical caller supplied `ExecutionTime` contract in [Runtime Integration Contract](../../Runtime_Integration_Contract.md). Component-specific age, expiry and timeout ownership remains defined by each unit contract.


### U-DEMAND-ARBITER

`U-DEMAND-ARBITER` is the one `SC-DEMAND.V` policy decision point. It consumes separately typed snapshots, never a heterogeneous collection: `AcceleratorPosition`, `BrakeState`, `ActiveRidingSettings` (the dedicated active projection; pending settings are excluded), `RidingAuthority`, `BatteryOperatingLimitsAssessment` with its assessment timestamp, `QualifiedMotion`, `ActualTractionOutput`, current platform context/health, and its immutable compiled `DemandPolicyCalibration` parameters. It emits `WheelTorqueRequest`; it neither activates settings, creates Session authority or a fault latch, evaluates battery protection, nor applies FOC/output control.

The command has `requestedWheelTorqueNewtonMetres: Real`, signed at the rear wheel: positive is forward propulsion and negative is regenerative braking. It also carries independent `maximumDischargeCurrentAmperes` and `maximumRegenerativeChargeCurrentAmperes`; these electrical allowances are forwarded to Traction for actual energy-transfer enforcement and do not determine wheel-torque sign. It carries `authorityGeneration`, `decisionSequence`, `decisionTimeMicroseconds`, `commandExpiryTimeMicroseconds`, producer/context and configuration identity, qualification and decision reason. Authority carries its own decision sequence/time and expiry. The command expiry is independently evaluated from authority expiry; matching authority generation is necessary but never extends either expiry. `U-TRACTION-ACCEPT` emits an accepted record with `effectiveAcceptedExpiryTimeMicroseconds=min(authorityExpiryTimeMicroseconds, commandExpiryTimeMicroseconds, acceptedAgeBound)` in the caller supplied monotonic domain; `U-TRACTION-CONTROL` rechecks that expiry using its `ExecutionTime`. A record is usable only when every required common contributing record is qualified, within its locally evaluated age bound, same current vehicle/reset generation, configuration-compatible and coherent at the declared decision snapshot. For `AcceleratorPosition`, measurement age is checked from `Position.sampleTimeMicroseconds`, while status and current source-health attribution come from `Qualification`. Any common producer, context, configuration/calibration or platform-health invalidation withdraws the prior command and diagnostic atomically; an unavailable electrical branch sets only its corresponding current allowance to zero and is not represented by its last value.

The arbitration is a bounded clamp, with separate non-negative propulsion and non-positive regeneration permissions. Pedal request maps through the active level profile to positive wheel torque. Level 0 allows no regeneration. Levels 1–5 use the parent provisional neutral at 35%; the compiled parameter set supplies their full and same-positive endpoints, ramps, taper and response parameters. Startup validation keeps the profile unavailable for missing, malformed or unvalidated required parameters. A lever blocks positive torque only; it may still permit accelerator-requested regeneration under the profile. The active speed limit and hard 40-km/h cutoff constrain both signs. `BatteryOperatingLimitsAssessment` carries independently qualified discharge and regenerative-charge amperes limits plus separate SOC positive-propulsion torque/speed caps. An unavailable electrical limit sets its allowance to zero; it does not directly select a wheel-torque sign. A low-SOC restriction constrains positive torque only. Demand forwards both electrical bounds with its wheel-torque request and never converts amperes to N m. Fault/authority withdrawal, missing common rider/motion/profile evidence, and every both-sign parent inhibition command zero. The approved 5-level exception omits comfort smoothing, but still applies the qualified low-speed taper. Demand does not manufacture a new rider choice, numerical threshold, ramp or taper.

Post-stop regeneration is a Demand-owned rearm state, initially disabled for each vehicle context and after qualified standstill. It remains disabled through hand-push movement. It re-arms only after the current `ActualTractionOutput` provides qualified evidence of forward travel together with qualified **positive applied wheel-torque** evidence in the same context/configuration; a commanded positive value, PWM state, Hall state/edge, speed alone, or a zero request cannot re-arm it. This rearm episode is separate from Demand's regenerative-restriction recovery episode: on each qualified partial or full restriction of rider-requested regeneration, Demand retains the reduced regenerative-charge permission/current allowance under that new internal episode generation. While the scooter remains moving and the accelerator remains continuously in the regenerative range, a later electrical-limit clearance or partial relaxation alone cannot increase the held allowance. Leaving the regenerative range or qualified standstill qualifies that episode before **or after** clearance; once both episode qualification and current qualified clearance/relaxation exist, Demand recovers automatically, including at standstill. BatteryProtection supplies current operating-limit assessments. Demand owns the regeneration recovery episode and post-stop rearm; SessionPolicy owns riding fault retention and restart.

For one decision snapshot, precedence is deterministic: (1) context/reset, platform-health, qualification/age/coherency failure in common rider/motion/profile evidence, expired or generation-mismatched authority, and both-sign parent inhibition withdraw both signs; (2) hard 40-km/h cutoff, active speed limit and qualified both-sign limits constrain both signs; (3) the post-stop/hand-push regeneration rearm gate and Demand-owned regeneration permission/recovery guards constrain **negative** torque only; (4) lever and SOC positive-torque/speed caps constrain **positive** torque only; (5) the active-level pedal/profile request and low-speed taper are clamped to the remaining sign-specific envelopes. An independently unavailable electrical allowance is zero and Traction enforces it in the actual current direction; it does not select a wheel-torque sign. If a simultaneous event withdraws a restriction while any tighter inhibition is present, the tighter inhibition wins; a recovery/increase never takes effect before the withdrawal/invalidation result in that snapshot. Equivalent sources must declare the same snapshot/coherency epoch; otherwise the decision is unavailable/zero, not ordered by transport arrival.

`DemandPolicyCalibration` is an owner-local immutable build parameter set, not an implicit constant or runtime dependency. It carries the level maps, full/end-point correspondence, neutral point, speed/taper shape, ramps, reference limits and symbolic response parameters used by the decision. A missing, malformed or unvalidated field makes the profile unavailable during startup/self-test. Its numerical values, bounds, units, owner, review state and verification are an end-to-end engineering gate: `SC-DEMAND` releases each parameter with the matching input qualification and traction acceptance/physical-response verification. Changing it requires rebuilding, deploying and restarting the software. Until that happens, the design is **not implementation-ready** and must not claim a response time, ramp, taper, numerical speed threshold or physical torque tracking bound.
