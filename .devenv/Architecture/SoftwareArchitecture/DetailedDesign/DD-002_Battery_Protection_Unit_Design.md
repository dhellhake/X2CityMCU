# DD-002 — Battery protection and BMS software-unit design

**Draft vehicle baseline — 2026-09-16.** Full BMS transaction/decode/publication, battery evaluation/restriction, reset invariants and acceptance vectors are retained in their owning component folders.

**Draft — 2026-09-13.** This split detailed design preserves the complete vehicle unit contracts for `SC-BMS-LINK.V` and `SC-BAT-POLICY.V`. It derives the two battery Abstract Software obligations now co-located in [BatteryControl](../../SystemArchitecture/Modelling/BatteryControl/README.md). Historical release identities and record counts remain historical; this successor neither selects electrical interfaces, hosts, numeric limits, physical protection, vendor internals, BMS writes, an immutable pack identity, or diagnostic coverage.

## Design-wide execution and data rules

BmsLink sends a separate reporting-only `bmsStatusOut` contribution to BatteryProtection's `bmsStatusIn` and U-BAT-POLICY-RESTRICTION; it never enters the evaluator or capability qualification. BmsLink privately retains startup compatibility failure in dependent operational field qualification until controller startup. The evaluator alone recognizes and retains the functional effect of restart-required faults from qualified operational evidence, producing both branch candidates unqualified with effective zero until controller startup. The restriction unit preserves candidate qualification, applies ordinary restrictions and never uses diagnostic status to decide limits.

Each typed battery record carries its stated semantic values, producer/reset context and field-specific qualification. Battery measurements and operating-limit assessments expose no source timestamp, assessment timestamp, or source-age field. This does not remove transaction receipt/order evidence or the deliberate fault-recognition ordering in `BatteryStatus`. `BmsLink` privately supervises accepted-source age, `BatteryProtection` privately supervises receipt of current `BmsLink` publications, and every downstream consumer privately supervises its own publication-delivery liveness using caller-supplied `ExecutionTime`. These independent timers do not transfer or renew one another's evidence. `Unknown`, `Invalid`, `Stale`, and `Qualified` are different states; a presentation fallback is never qualified data.

Battery supervision carries separate private timeout limits for every required source field and corresponding update-latency uncertainty budget; BMS transaction response; startup/reset requalification; BmsLink-to-BatteryProtection publication delivery; BatteryProtection-to-Session/Demand and BatteryProtection-to-HMI publication delivery; and consumer use/effect latency. SessionEligibility and Demand each watch operating-limits and actual-SOC publications independently; Demand's two watchdogs remain separate from each other and from BmsLink source-age timers. HMI watches actual SOC and signed current independently. These limits are private compiled integration parameters, not payload timestamps, sequences, or age values. Numeric values and end-to-end margins must be derived and accepted against captured selected BMS behavior, polling/service schedule, task WCET/blocking, consumer use/effect latency, queue/delivery bound, and conservative source-validity/update/detection uncertainty before implementation. On host/consumer startup or reset, each receiver begins unqualified with no usable prior snapshot; reset does not start a valid timeout window. The receiver must complete bounded post-reset resynchronization and receive a current publication before delivery liveness is established. Source timeout qualification loss enters the existing required-information/reason logic; where that logic recognizes a fault, BatteryStatus preserves the existing recognition order. Since remote BMS sample time and internal update latency are not known from a host reply, the accepted source-validity budget must account for that uncertainty and cannot claim a fabricated BMS time. A newly accepted matching source response refreshes only its private producer timer; a newly published snapshot may refresh a consumer's private delivery watchdog even when unchanged, but cannot restore source qualification. Qualified zero remains valid data and does not mean timeout.

The vehicle component instances `SC-BMS-LINK.V` and `SC-BAT-POLICY.V` execute on `HC-CONTROLLER` with independent producer/reset contexts. `SC-RIDING-CONTROL.V`, through U-DEMAND-ARBITER, permits positive propulsion only above 10% qualified actual SOC. This does not alter BatteryProtection source qualification, current-limit, or fault responsibilities.
