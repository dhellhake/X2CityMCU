# MCD-001 — Hall-sensored traction-control technical design

**Draft 1.2 — 2026-09-14; successor to MCD-001-R1.0.** This is the canonical selection and technical design for the installed rear hub motor. It records the selected EVM traction-board baseline and WeAct STM32H723VGT6 vehicle host, not an energized procedure, pin allocation, calibration, numeric limit, physical protection or qualified vehicle capability. [MOT-001](MOT-001_Motor_Interface_Evidence.md) remains the canonical record of the supplied measurements.

## Decision and applicability

The selected traction method is **three-phase Hall-sensored sinusoidal field-oriented control (FOC)** for the unknown permanent-magnet direct-drive hub motor. It uses the existing signed rear-wheel-torque request as its rider-facing abstraction and realizes the permitted request as a signed q-axis current request. It has no rider speed servo, no position servo, no stationary holding torque and no normal-start forced rotor alignment, open-loop rotation or sensorless fallback.

This selection fits the owner-defined Levels 0–5: Level 0 has positive torque over its full accelerator travel and no one-pedal regeneration; Levels 1–5 may request signed torque about their virtual neutral. FOC offers a common current-controlled path for smooth positive and negative torque modulation, while the installed Hall inputs retain a position source at rest and low speed. It does **not** establish sinusoidal back-EMF, torque smoothness, torque constant, current limit, braking availability, field orientation accuracy or performance.

Hall-sensored six-step control was considered but not selected because its six fixed stator orientations cause torque ripple and coarser transitions for the required graded regenerative behaviour. Sensorless FOC was not selected as a sole or automatic fallback because BEMF observability is weak at zero/low speed and it cannot establish standstill/rollback rotor angle without a separate transition design. The established Hall path avoids both an unqualified sensorless launch and an added Hall-to-observer handover.

Primary technical references: [Microchip AN4064, Hall-sensored FOC for a scooter hub motor](https://www.microchip.com/content/dam/mchp/documents/MCU16/ApplicationNotes/ApplicationNotes/Sensored-Hall-Effect-Sensor-Based-Field-Oriented-Control-of-Three-Phase-BLDC-Motor-Using-dsPIC33CK-DS00004064.pdf), [TI MSPM0 sensored-FOC guide](https://www.ti.com/lit/ug/slau948/slau948.pdf), [ST motor-control ecosystem](https://www.st.com/content/st_com/en/ecosystems/stm32-motor-control-ecosystem.html), and [TI six-step overview](https://www.ti.com/document-viewer/lit/html/SLAAE95A/GUID-46F248D2-1B32-4D5F-82E0-D1509641BB4F). They support the general approach only; neither provides the motor-specific map, gains, limits or safety evidence required here.

## Boundaries, quantities and sign conventions

`SC-DEMAND.V` remains the owner of an allowed signed **wheel-torque request** `T_w*` (N m); it is not a speed command. `SC-TRACTION-CTRL.V` accepts it only with current authority and all existing validity/expiry/context rules. Its calibrated internal request is

`Iq* = clamp(K_Tinv(T_w*), Iq_min(conditions), Iq_max(conditions))` in A, and `Id* = 0 A`.

`K_Tinv` is a configuration-qualified inverse torque map, not the unverified 0.56-ohm lead-pair reading or a generic motor constant. Positive `Iq` is defined during commissioning to produce **forward wheel torque**; negative `Iq` may produce regenerative braking in forward motion. The signed reference says nothing by itself about battery current or energy direction. Actual mechanical power is `P_mech,actual = T_actual × omega_w`; a forward-torque request during backward rolling can make that value negative and therefore can be a generating case, but request alone proves neither torque nor generation. Mechanical generation/braking is not automatically net battery charging: copper/inverter losses and qualified path routing can absorb power. Battery charge acceptance uses qualified DC-link/pack current or power and path observations, with their latency and uncertainty, rather than `Iq` or `T_actual` sign. Any traction state predicted or observed to charge the DC link/pack is subject to the same qualified charge-acceptance and bus-voltage policy as ordinary negative regenerative demand. It must clip or withdraw current as that envelope requires; it must not let rollback bypass it.

The angle is electrical `theta_e` (rad), electrical speed is `omega_e` (rad/s), phase currents are `i_a`, `i_b`, `i_c` (A) and satisfy `i_a+i_b+i_c=0` only after measurement qualification. Clarke/Park processing forms `i_d`, `i_q` (A). `Vdc` is the qualified inverter DC-link voltage (V); `Vd*`, `Vq*` are d/q voltage requests (V); duty is dimensionless. Vehicle speed and wheel angular speed remain separately qualified motion observations. Hall-edge timing cannot silently replace the independent control-speed calibration.

## Electrical feedback and PWM realization

The selected DRV8300DRGE-EVM supplies the three-phase bridge and three low-side 3-mΩ/10-V/V current channels, but the physical traction path still requires a host/adapter complementary-PWM contract, independently acting hardware-fault shutdown augmentation and an output-observation/protection path. The EVM has no local PVDD/GVDD/gross-current comparator/gate clamp: its `FAULT` header signal is host-driven LED only, and DRV8300 provides bootstrap/GVDD UVLO only. The selected practical current topology is **three phase-leg low-side shunts**, one in each inverter leg, each with a differential amplifier and ADC channel. It gives phase-current feedback for all three legs while avoiding a single DC-link shunt's vector reconstruction ambiguity. The phase current is observable from a given shunt only while that leg's low-side device is conducting; the controller never relabels an unavailable shunt observation as a phase current. TI's 25-A software-limited continuous-drive condition and the EVM thermal/current envelope constrain, rather than establish, the eventual current envelope.

Use centre-aligned PWM with space-vector modulation (SVPWM). Let `Ts` be the engineering-selected and verified PWM period. One timer trigger occurs in every `Ts`, at a preloaded position inside the next commanded SVPWM/voltage-vector sector and duty pattern's proven common low-side window. It starts two simultaneous dual-ADC ranks: rank 1 supplies the selected independent phase-current pair for FOC; rank 2 supplies the remaining direct current and Vdc 4.733 µs later. A Hall sector supplies rotor angle but does not establish conducting shunts. A low-side phase sample is usable only when its gate state has been continuously low-side-on for at least `t_blank + t_settle + t_acq`, and the current amplifier/ADC has not flagged saturation; its record carries phase identity, commanded-vector/ADC-context identity, sign convention, carrier epoch and valid-window evidence. The modulator enforces the required rank-1 window through duty clamping/vector limitation. If it cannot obtain the selected independent valid pair in the carrier epoch, it limits the vector to a valid sampling region; if that still fails, it declares the fast sample unavailable and withdraws torque request. The third phase is computed as `i_c = -(i_a + i_b)` only for the qualified simultaneous rank-1 pair; direct rank-2 third-current comparison is retained for offset/plausibility diagnostics. `Ts`, `t_blank`, `t_settle`, `t_acq`, amplifier range, shunt value, commanded-vector windows and permissible modulation range are verified hardware/configuration parameters.

At each completed valid sample, the fast current-control cycle shall: validate the sample/context; Clarke/Park it using the current `theta_e`; regulate d/q currents; limit the requested voltage vector to the presently usable modulation boundary; inverse Park/Clarke; and write buffered compare values for application at the following carrier update. The complete ADC JEOS acquisition, control computation and register latch must finish before its allocated next-carrier latch deadline; any late/missing update is a fast-loop failure, not permission to repeat a stale nonzero vector. ADC sampling, deadline, PWM update point, dead-time distortion, offset calibration and hardware over-current response have explicit board contracts before implementation acceptance. The controller host is the owner-selected WeAct STM32H723VGT6; its selected TIM8/TIM2, ADC1/ADC2, regular-DMA, pin, power and independent-inhibit contract is [allocated here](../Architecture/DRV8300DRGE-EVM/DRV8300DRGE-EVM_WeAct_STM32H723VGT6_Traction_HSI.md). Its numeric configuration is an engineering target pending the stated electrical/timing/thermal qualification, not a completed FOC acceptance.

Each current PI uses `Vd,u = Kp_d × (Id*−Id) + Xi_d` and `Vq,u = Kp_q × (Iq*−Iq) + Xi_q`; `Kp` has V/A and `Ki` has V/(A s), with `dXi/dt = Ki × current_error` before anti-windup. The two axes apply back-calculation or conditional integration against the **same vector saturation result** used by the modulator. A d/q-vector limit, rather than independent uncoordinated axis clamps, preserves the available voltage boundary. Gains and anti-windup tracking gains are derived from measured motor/bridge dynamics and `Ts`; no numeric value is selected.

The PI state initializes/holds at a qualified zero-output state on new context, startup, authority withdrawal, invalid angle/sample/voltage/configuration, speed cutoff, hardware-fault event and shutdown. Re-entry after a valid current configuration must start from a bounded/bump-less state derived from present measurements and the currently admissible vector; a stale integral must never produce a nonzero torque vector. A bad current sample, impossible reconstruction, stale `Vdc`, PWM-update miss, loss of the hardware fault path, invalid rotor estimate or invalid control configuration makes the torque-realization request unavailable/zero and reports the observation state. Initial/incomplete qualification merely withholds torque unless the existing rules classify the condition as a recognized fault; there is no universal Hall fault. Exact classification, detection coverage and response times require qualification.

The permitted magnitude envelope is applied before `K_Tinv` and again as a current/vector limit. Its priority is: both-sign withdrawal (authority, shutdown, recognized fault, speed cutoff, unavailable required fast-loop/configuration evidence); then physical bridge/current/voltage protection; then qualified DC-link/pack charge or discharge and thermal envelopes; then the existing positive-only SOC restriction and rider/profile request. It may only reduce `|Iq*|` toward zero; it shall not change the requested torque sign, increase torque magnitude, create a negative `Id*`, or create regenerative torque to satisfy a voltage condition. The limit controller may be a bounded clamp or a separately qualified feedback limiter, but its output must remain within the presently safe envelope and its state obeys the same reset/anti-windup rules.

For every fast voltage/current limit, derive a bound `t_limit = t_sample_age + t_convert + t_compute + t_latch + t_bridge` and an uncertainty/current-rise/headroom bound such that the protected electrical boundary cannot be crossed before the response takes effect. `t_sample_age` includes only the accepted same-epoch sample, and BMS UART age is never substituted. The hardware fault path covers faults faster than the software bound or when that bound cannot be met. A later qualification records the allowable DC-link/current limit, prediction/measurement uncertainty, required headroom, current slew, `Ts` scheduling terms and independently observed physical response.

## Hall rotor observation and direction

`HC-MOTOR → HC-INPUT-FE → U-HALL-CAPTURE/SC-HALL-IF.V → U-HALL-POSITION → SC-TRACTION-CTRL.V` is the required signal path. Hall interface owns capture integrity, sampled-state association, map/alignment, legal transition, direction, timestamp and the complete qualified electrical position; traction only consumes that nonqueued result. The same fast control chain receives `U-MOTOR-PHASE-FAST` calibrated phase feedback and `U-SUPPLY-FAST` locally measured `Vdc`; rank-1 current pair is same aperture and rank-2 Vdc is 4.733 µs later. BMS UART voltage/current remains slower battery-policy/diagnostic evidence and cannot substitute for either fast-loop measurement. The EVM default Hall rail is 3.3 V, but its R9/R10/R11 pull Hall outputs to fixed 3.3 V independently of `HALL_PWR`. Fit J3 for `HALL_EXT` and provide a qualified external 5-V Hall supply while retaining those 3.3-V output pull-ups, matching the existing motor observation. Qualify common return, thresholds and unpowered behavior; this does not establish connector pin order, output topology, pull-up limits, phase association, code order, 60/120-degree arrangement, pole-pair count, offset, or fault coverage.

A commissioned motor configuration supplies the allowed three-bit Hall code sequence, transition direction, Hall-sector electrical boundaries, phase permutation/polarity, electrical offset, interpolation policy, pole pairs and validity limits. At rest, a current qualified static Hall state and that qualified configuration may supply a **bounded sector estimate** without waiting for an edge. It neither proves standstill nor provides a precise rotor angle. Its midpoint uncertainty and Hall placement/offset/sampling errors create d-axis current and torque error even though `Id* = 0`; launch torque/current limits must include that worst credible angle error. Normal startup may command propulsion only after this configuration and its static-sector launch bounds are qualified. It must not rotate, align or hunt merely to discover them.

While moving, the estimator may interpolate within the last validated sector from Hall transition time and estimated electrical speed, subject to an explicit age/acceleration/angle-error bound. A transition corrects the estimate through a bounded blend or correction rule; it must not create an unconstrained phase jump. Direction is inferred only from a qualified sequence and interval; edge absence is interpreted by operating condition and cannot by itself diagnose a stationary motor. Stale, illegal, out-of-sequence, context-mismatched or map-incompatible evidence invalidates the traction angle for current control. The resulting torque request is withdrawn under the accepted authority/fault contract; it is never replaced by sensorless, forced rotation or a guessed code table.

## Current control, state behavior and energy policy

The selected baseline is surface-PM-style `Id* = 0`: no MTPA or field weakening is selected. `Iq*` is issued only after the accepted authority/command, a valid Hall angle, valid current/voltage feedback, a qualified active configuration and the existing Ready, brake, SOC, temperature, speed and fault conditions. In particular:

| Situation | Traction-control action |
|---|---|
| Ready at rest, valid calibrated Hall sector, positive demand | Start bounded forward-torque current control. No alignment, open-loop ramp, automatic wheel motion or holding current is used. |
| Backward rolling, positive demand | Apply only the commissioning-proven forward-torque `Iq*`, subject to current and DC-link limits. Use qualified DC-link/pack current or power plus routing and latency evidence to determine whether charge-acceptance restriction applies; a conservative limitation is permitted only with its stated basis. It is not a free propulsion exception. |
| Forward rolling, negative demand | Apply negative `Iq*` only through the existing regeneration permissives, low-speed taper, post-stop gate and charge/DC-link limits. |
| Standstill or parking/pushing | Request zero regenerative torque; no hold. Valid positive launch follows existing Ready/profile rules. |
| Brake input, lost authority, invalid/stale command, invalid angle/current/configuration, fault or shutdown | Request zero torque of both signs; publish acceptance, PWM/gate state and independently qualified output/energy observations. Existing recognized fault recovery remains clean restart only. |
| Active lower/40 km/h speed boundary | Request zero torque of both signs. This is a command withdrawal, never intentional active overspeed braking. Falling below the boundary follows the parent positive-resumption and separate regeneration rules. |

The active-electrical-braking output used by the existing rear-light policy shall mean **observed decelerating wheel torque**: a qualified physical output estimate establishes `T_actual × omega_w < 0`, or an independently qualified decelerating-torque observation establishes the same outcome. `T_actual` may be estimated from measured phase currents, qualified rotor angle and the commissioned motor map, with stated validity/error bounds; no direct torque sensor is selected. It shall not be asserted merely because `Iq* < 0`, an inverter gate is enabled, or a negative battery current is expected. Consequently, positive commissioned forward torque while the wheel rolls backward may be active electrical braking and may trigger the existing brake-light policy. The torque/output estimate, wheel-direction evidence and uncertainty bound are separate HW/SW qualification gates.

“PWM off”, gate-off or zero `Iq*` is not a proof of zero motor torque, DC-link current, generated energy or wheel restraint. At speed, bridge body diodes, freewheel paths, back-EMF, failed gates and residual DC-link energy can create effects after command withdrawal. `HC-TRACTION-POWER` must provide a qualified physical outcome/protection contract, including externally driven wheel cases and charge-ineligible/high-SOC cases. `SC-TRACTION-CTRL.V` records that observed outcome; it does not infer it from its own command.

## 40 km/h voltage-headroom feasibility gate

The owner target remains just below 40 km/h; it is not reduced by this design. The reported `Kv = 9.5 rpm/V` is unverified. A 16-inch nominal wheel has a nominal circumference of about 1.276 m, so 40 km/h is about 522 rpm. If the reported constant has the ordinary no-load mechanical interpretation, it predicts about **55 V** (`522 / 9.5`) before load, copper loss, inverter drop, sag or modulation reserve. That exceeds the 14S nominal 50.4 V and can also exceed the low-SOC loaded DC-link condition; the 58.8-V outer-cell reference does not demonstrate the required range.

Accordingly, the design establishes a feasibility gate: characterize motor back-EMF/Kv, phase resistance/inductance, phase map, loaded battery/inverter voltage and modulation reserve across the intended SOC/temperature/load envelope; then demonstrate positive torque and required control margin just below 40 km/h. The baseline does **not** select field weakening as an unqualified means to pass. If the gate fails, performance remains unproven and a controlled engineering/requirements decision is required before a later restricted field-weakening design, motor/inverter change or other performance impact can be considered. A successful no-load test or full-charge-only result is insufficient.

## Calibration, diagnostics and verification

Commissioning is a controlled off-road engineering activity, not normal startup. Before any vehicle acceptance, establish and identify the map/version for phase permutation/polarity; Hall code order and placement; electrical offset and sector error; pole pairs; current-sensor polarity/offset/scale; `Vdc` scale; current/thermal/bus limits; torque map; PWM/sample timing; PI gains; output-observation semantics; and permissible operating envelope. `U-PLATFORM-CALIBRATION` in `SC-PLATFORM.V` alone activates this complete bundle while disarmed, atomically gives it a new configuration context and invalidates all old current, Vdc, Hall and command records; `U-PLATFORM-BINDING` applies static resources only under that active context. No live partial update or default map is usable. The motor temperature resistance observation in MOT-001 does not qualify a motor temperature feedback channel.

Service information must expose current configuration identity, qualified Hall/current/voltage/angle states, accepted `T_w*`/`Iq*`/`Id*`, limiting cause, PWM/gate request, DC-link current/voltage where measured and separately qualified actual-output/energy observation. These are current-session observations, not retained fault history. A calibration mismatch or unavailable required configuration cannot be treated as a default map.

Verification must cover at minimum: phase/Hall permutations and both directions; static-sector launch on a slope/rollback boundary; angle error and Hall transition seams; current-sensor offset/polarity/saturation; current-loop saturation and anti-windup; current/PWM timing misses; positive, negative and mechanically generating rollback energy cases; charge-ineligible/high-SOC DC-link response; both-sign speed-boundary withdrawal with externally driven wheel; post-stop regeneration gate; fault/restart inhibition; actual-output observation versus requested zero; and the 40-km/h headroom gate. These tests derive their safe fixture, current/voltage, containment and response bounds before energization. This document records none as executed.

## Traceability and open boundaries

The design refines [DEC-MOTOR-001](../Requirements/DEC-001_Decisions_and_Open_Issues.md#dec-motor-001), [REQ-SYS-TRQCTL-001](../Requirements/Abstract_Software_Requirements/Detailed_Design_Interface_Allocations.md#req-sys-trqctl-001), [UR-TRACTION-CONTROL-001](../Requirements/Unit_Requirements/Control_Unit_Requirements.md#ur-traction-control-001), HSI-001/004, INT-004, the existing torque/regeneration/speed requirements and HW-I-003. It leaves MOT-001 observations, all released parent requirements and all numerical calibration/protection/physical-acceptance gates intact.

Open boundaries include motor type/waveform, Hall geometry/map/alignment, pole count, winding topology, `Kv`/`Kt`, inductance, insulation, thermal sensor/limits, inverter rating/protection, bridge safe-off/generated-energy behavior, gains, torque/current limits, vehicle-speed calibration and fault coverage. The host/EVM resource contract, current/Vdc realization and PWM timing are allocated to the traction HSI. No hardware was changed or energized.
